\title{\textbf{BOREAS: Borehole Array Storage}\\[6pt]
\large A downcomer geometry and a conducting formation for
latent-heat storage in repurposed wellbores}
\author{J.\ R.\ Barbosa}
\date{Model version 1.0}

\begin{abstract}
BOREAS replaces two assumptions carried by the THUMS/P2H2P model through
sixteen versions: the hairpin tube geometry and the adiabatic borehole wall
(hypothesis H6). The two are not independent. A hairpin requires its melting
cascade to be graded along the \emph{tube path} rather than along depth, which
places two melting temperatures at the same depth in contiguous phase-change
material; the resulting leg-to-leg conduction is \SI{92}{\kilo\watt} against a
duty of order \SI{120}{\kilo\watt}, a short circuit across the very cascade the
model represents, and a term the model never contained. It also makes the
axial coordinate a path length rather than a depth, so a depth-dependent
formation temperature cannot be attached at all. Replacing the hairpin with
$\nft$ downward fin tubes and a single insulated downcomer removes both
obstacles at equal pressure drop and equal storage volume. With the wall
conducting, the storage efficiency ceases to be unity by construction and
becomes a measurement. Field shielding is represented through a depth-dependent
well spacing $B(z)$ appropriate to directionally drilled island fields, where
wellheads sit on \SI{1.83}{\metre} centres and diverge to \SI{70}{\metre} or
more at depth. Loss falls from \SI{75.6}{\percent} of charge for an isolated
well to \SI{0.93}{\percent} for a field at island spacing.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why a new series}

P2H2P/THUMS is frozen at v0.16 and remains runnable; nothing in BOREAS edits
it, and the build refuses to proceed if the THUMS sources have moved. The
separation is deliberate: the geometry change is not a refinement of the
hairpin model but a different machine, and the two should be comparable rather
than one overwriting the other.

\section{The hairpin is self-contradictory}
\label{sec:hairpin}

Charging water enters the borehole at $\Tm + \Delta T_{4C\text{-}M}$ and glides
$\Delta T_{3C\text{-}2C}$ as it gives up heat, so the \emph{return} leg of a
hairpin runs approximately \SI{45}{\kelvin} below the melting temperature.
Graded by depth, that leg refreezes material the down leg has just melted.

The model therefore graded the cascade along the developed tube path. This is
self-consistent, but it requires two distinct melting temperatures to coexist
at the same depth, separated by centimetres of \emph{contiguous} phase-change
material with no intervening wall. Treating the two legs as parallel cylinders
in a medium of conductivity $k_{\mathrm{PCM}}$, the conduction shape factor
gives

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
hairpins & leg spacing & leg-to-leg conduction & fraction of duty \\
\midrule
1 & \SI{126}{\milli\metre} & \SI{43}{\kilo\watt} & \SI{36}{\percent} \\
2 & \SI{89}{\milli\metre}  & \SI{62}{\kilo\watt} & \SI{52}{\percent} \\
3 & \SI{73}{\milli\metre}  & \SI{92}{\kilo\watt} & \SI{77}{\percent} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Parasitic conduction between the two legs of a hairpin, which the
THUMS model does not contain. Note that it grows with the number of
hairpins, which the parametric study of v0.16 ranked as the single most
beneficial design variable.}
\end{table}

Two further consequences follow. Path-graded material is not pourable: it
would require radially segregated cartridges, a manufacturing requirement
never stated. And since each depth is occupied by two tube segments, one rock
temperature would have two claimants, so no depth-dependent boundary condition
can be applied.

\section{The downcomer geometry}

BOREAS separates the two functions. All $\nft$ fin tubes run downward and
exchange heat; a single \emph{insulated downcomer} returns the whole flow
without exchanging. They meet in a manifold at the bottom of the same hole:
no lateral, no new drilling, and the same class of object as the U-bend it
replaces.

Sizing the downcomer bore for equal velocity,
\begin{equation}
r_{\mathrm{id}} = r_i \sqrt{\nft},
\end{equation}
gives the same velocity in every passage over the same total path $2L_{w}$, so
the pressure drop is that of the hairpin it replaces. A nested coaxial
arrangement, in which the return runs inside each fin tube, preserves storage
volume equally well but divides the flow area and costs a factor $3.3$ in
pumping --- the single quantity the v0.16 study identified as governing
subsurface efficiency.

The downcomer is run off-centre. A concentric arrangement does not close for
three \SI{1.25}{inch} tubes in a \SI{7}{inch} hole; the offset arrangement
packs with clearance for $\nft \le 5$.

\subsection{The axial coordinate becomes a depth}

With the return no longer exchanging, $L_{\mathrm{tube}} = L_{w}$ rather than
$2L_{w}$, and the solver coordinate maps one-to-one onto depth. On charge the
water reaches the bottom of the hole through the downcomer before it has given
up any heat, so $z = 0$ is the bottom and the cascade necessarily runs
\emph{hot at the bottom}. On discharge the flow reverses and the march is run
mirrored, as is the melting-temperature profile.

\subsection{The cascade aligns with the geothermal gradient}

The formation is coldest at the surface and hottest at depth. A hot-at-top
cascade therefore opposes the gradient at both ends. The net driving
difference is identical for either orientation --- the mean of $\Tm(z)$ is
unchanged --- but the distribution is not:

\centering

at \SI{8000}{ft}, \SI{56.5}{\kelvin\per\kilo\metre}
 & $\Delta T$ at top & $\Delta T$ at bottom & cannot freeze \\

hot-at-top (THUMS)     & $+132$\,K & $-55$\,K & \SI{29}{\percent} \\

\section{The formation}

\subsection{The thermal circuit}

The path from the phase-change material to undisturbed rock is a series chain
per unit length of borehole,
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{tot}} = R_{\mathrm{PCM}} + R_{\mathrm{casing}}
  + R_{\mathrm{cement}} + \Rg(t),
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{PCM}} = \frac{\ln(R_{c}/r_e)}{2\pi\,\nft\,k_{\mathrm{PCM}}},
\qquad
\Rg(t) = \frac{1}{2\pi k_r}
  \ln\!\left(\frac{2.5\sqrt{\alpha t}}{r_b}\right).
\end{equation}

At the design point the terms are \SI{25}{\percent}, \SI{0.1}{\percent},
\SI{5}{\percent} and \SI{70}{\percent} respectively. The casing is thermally
invisible and the cement is a rounding error: the ground resistance is the
model, and unlike the others it grows with time. A quoted loss is therefore
meaningless without the age of the field beside it.

\subsection{The gradient is independent of the source temperature}

The wellhead temperature of the geothermal stream is a \emph{lower bound} on
the formation temperature at producing depth: the water cools ascending the
production string, and the producing wells may be deeper than the retrofits.
Deriving one quantity from the other writes that bound as an equality and
discards the physics the deficit represents. Both are specified, and only the
inequality
\begin{equation}
T_{\mathrm{source}} \;\le\; T_{\mathrm{surf}}
  + \nabla T \, L_{\mathrm{prod}}
\end{equation}
is enforced, as a check that can fail.

\subsection{Coupling to the enthalpy march}

Two linear sinks act on each node: the tube at $T_0$ with conductance $K$, and
the formation at $\Trock$ with conductance $K_\ell$. These combine exactly,
\begin{equation}
K(T_0 - T) + K_\ell(\Trock - T) = (K + K_\ell)(T_{\mathrm{eq}} - T),
\qquad
T_{\mathrm{eq}} = \frac{K T_0 + K_\ell \Trock}{K + K_\ell},
\end{equation}
so the segment advance remains implicit in the material temperature and
unchanged in form. The split is recovered afterwards from the net heat. Adding
the loss explicitly after the step would be a half-order scheme on a term that
reverses sign with depth.

\section{Field shielding: $B(z)$}

\subsection{The field is a cone}

Wellheads in a directionally drilled island field sit on \SI{1.83}{\metre}
(\SI{6}{ft}) centres, and the wells diverge with drift angles reaching
\SI{84}{\degree}. Spacing is therefore a function of depth, not a number. The
governing timescale is that for neighbouring thermal fields to merge,
$r^2/\alpha$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
spacing & merge time \\
\midrule
\SI{1.83}{\metre}  & \SI{36}{days} \\
\SI{8}{\metre}     & \SI{1.9}{yr}  \\
\SI{162}{\metre}   & \SI{779}{yr}  \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The shallow section is a single thermal body within a month; the deep section
is a field of isolated wells. Both occur in the same well.

The two effects oppose one another with depth: shallow rock is cold, so the
driving difference is largest exactly where shielding is strongest, while deep
rock is hot, so it is smallest exactly where the wells stand alone. This
partial cancellation is specific to directionally drilled island fields and
does not arise in purpose-drilled borehole storage, where spacing is uniform by
design.

\subsection{Model}

Above the kickoff point the wells are vertical; below it they fan at an
effective half-angle $\theta$, so
\begin{equation}
R_f(z) = R_0 + \max(0,\, z - z_{\mathrm{kop}})\tan\theta,
\qquad
B(z) = B_0 \, R_f(z)/R_0 .
\end{equation}
A shielded well loses only its share of the cluster perimeter flux, which
written as a resistance is
\begin{equation}
\Rper(z) = \frac{N\sqrt{\pi\alpha t}}{2\pi k_r R_f(z)} .
\end{equation}
This carries no driving temperature and is therefore a true resistance. The
two regimes are combined by taking the larger,
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{form}}(z) = R_{\mathrm{near}} + \max\!\big(\Rg,\, \Rper(z)\big),
\end{equation}
which is not a smooth blend and does not pretend to be: a well cannot lose more
than if it stood alone, a field cannot lose more than its perimeter admits, and
whichever constraint binds governs. The crossover is sharp in reality also ---
it is the moment the cells merge.

\section{Verification}

Each new check was corrupted deliberately and watched to fail. Neither of the
two below is sufficient alone, and the second exists because the first was
silent.

\paragraph{The adiabatic limit.} Driving $k_r \to 0$ must recover hypothesis H6
exactly. On its first execution this test returned a storage efficiency of
$0.20$ and a loss \emph{larger} than the physical case: the ground resistance
returned zero where it intended infinity when the line source fell outside its
validity, and zero resistance is infinite conductance. The test now passes with
zero loss and unit storage efficiency to the cyclic convergence tolerance.

\paragraph{The energy closure cannot detect a sign error.} Negating the loss
term leaves the residual at $5\times 10^{-15}$, because the tube duty and the
accumulated loss both carry the error and it cancels algebraically. The closure
reads only its own inputs. The loss therefore carries an independent check on
the direction of heat flow,
\begin{equation}
\Sigma = \sum q_{\mathrm{loss}}\,(T_{\mathrm{PCM}} - \Trock)
  \;=\; \sum K_\ell (\Delta T)^2 \;\ge\; 0 ,
\end{equation}
which is negative under a flipped convention.

\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
corruption & closure & $\Sigma$ \\
\midrule
(none)                           & $2.9\times10^{-16}$ & $+1.64\times10^{11}$ \\
loss omitted from the tube duty  & $5.6\times10^{-1}$ \textbf{fires} & $+1.75\times10^{11}$ silent \\
loss sign reversed               & $5.0\times10^{-15}$ silent & $-3.87\times10^{11}$ \textbf{fires} \\

This also settles the question of insulating the storage from the casing. For
an isolated well there is a shallow optimum near \SI{5}{\milli\metre}, worth
approximately \SI{19}{\percent} in delivered energy before the volume penalty
reverses it. In a field the loss is already below \SI{1}{\percent} and
insulation trades roughly \SI{11}{\percent} of the storage volume for nothing.
That an optimum exists at all is a symptom that the isolated-well scope is
unphysical: a model whose recommendation is to insulate is reporting on a
configuration that would not be built.

\section{Limitations}

The ground resistance is evaluated at a single field age; the decay of loss
from the first year to the thirtieth is obtained by repeated evaluation rather
than by an outer loop over years. The near-well term $R_{\mathrm{PCM}}$ treats
the tubes as independent parallel paths and ignores their crowding near the
casing; it is a quarter of a total the ground dominates, so refining it would
move the result less than the uncertainty in $k_r$. The surface machinery is
unchanged and frozen: round-trip efficiency is governed by the heat-pump lift,
$(T_h - T_0)/(T_h - T_{\mathrm{src}})$, and therefore by the geothermal source
temperature rather than by anything below ground.
