Only the two leftmost rows depend on $\Rg$ at all. Under the $\max$ rule of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:maxrule} the perimeter term binds at every depth for both
thousand-well cases and for the array, so those three results are independent
of the line-source prefactor; the ground term binds over the whole well for
the isolated case and over \SI{46}{\percent} of it at $N=100$.

Three observations.

\paragraph{The bounding cases differ by two orders of magnitude.} The
isolated-well figure is not a pessimistic estimate of the loss; it is a
different configuration, and one that would not be built. Reporting it alone
would make the concept appear unviable on the strength of an assumption nobody
made deliberately.

\paragraph{The realistic case is material but not fatal.} A thousand wells
fanning at \SI{45}{\degree} lose \SI{12.8}{\percent} of the charge, and the
island array at realistic packing loses \SIrange{9}{11}{\percent} --- the two
are the same machine within the uncertainty. Either costs
\SIrange{9}{15}{\percent} relative on round-trip efficiency against the
adiabatic value: large enough that omitting it would have been a genuine
defect, small enough that the concept survives.

An earlier version of this work reported \SI{0.93}{\percent} for the array and
concluded that a shallow, well-shielded interval beat a deep, exposed one.
That conclusion rested on the disc assumption and does not survive its
removal.

\paragraph{The fan angle matters nearly as much as the well count.} Steepening
from \SI{45}{\degree} to \SI{70}{\degree} more than doubles the loss, because
the wells separate faster with depth. In a real field that angle is set by
where the reservoir lies rather than by choice, which makes it a \emph{site}
parameter rather than a design one --- and reframes the question from ``what is
the loss'' to ``over what range of sites does this work''.

\subsection{Insulating the material from the casing}

For an isolated well there is a shallow optimum near \SI{5}{\milli\metre} of
annular insulation, worth some \SI{13}{\percent} in delivered energy before
the volume penalty reverses it; at \SI{20}{\milli\metre} the design is
destroyed outright, since the insulation is removed from the outermost and
largest-area annulus of material. In a field at realistic packing the loss is
already down to \SIrange{9}{11}{\percent}, so insulation trades roughly
\SI{11}{\percent} of the storage volume for a few points at best.

The sharper observation is that the optimum's \emph{existence} is a symptom
that the isolated-well scope is unphysical. A model whose recommendation is to
insulate is reporting on a configuration that would not be built. Insulation
moves the isolated well from \SI{79}{\percent} lost to \SI{71}{\percent} lost;
placing it in a field moves it to \SIrange{9}{11}{\percent}. These are not
comparable levers.
